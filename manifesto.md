# Ensemble — Founding Manifesto

The people building AI are not the ones being asked what it should become.

The most consequential technology of our time is being shaped by two separate conversations that rarely meet. In policy circles and philosophy departments, urgent questions are being raised about safety, alignment, governance, and the future of human agency. In the labs and engineering teams where the systems are actually being built, the focus is on capability, scale, and performance. Both conversations matter. But they are happening apart, and the gap between them is where the most dangerous oversights live.

Ensemble exists to close that gap. We are a London-based research and community organisation working at the intersection of AI safety, governance, and the philosophy of artificial intelligence. We produce original research that speaks across disciplinary lines. And we create spaces where researchers, engineers, policymakers, and the broader public think together about what is being built and what it means: through public talks, seminars, and a growing network across London's universities and institutions.

Our founding team spans technical AI research, psychology and law, and public policy. We believe the most useful thinking about AI comes from people willing to work across the boundaries that convention has drawn. We are not a policy shop that treats the technology as a black box. We are not a lab that treats governance as someone else's problem. We sit in the space between, and we think that space is where the most important work will be done.

## What we do

**Research.** We produce work that bridges technical AI safety and governance: the kind of analysis that is legible to both an engineer and a policymaker. Our focus areas are AI safety, AI governance, and the philosophy of AI. We do not produce position papers for their own sake. We produce research that changes how people think about what they are building.

**Events.** We run a regular programme of public talks, seminars, and roundtables in London. Our events are open to anyone: researchers, students, professionals, and people who are simply paying attention to what AI is becoming. Our speaker programme draws on leading researchers from UCL and across London's AI research community, alongside voices from policy, law, psychology, philosophy, and industry. Some events are large and open. Others are smaller, closed-door working sessions on specific problems. All of them are built around the conviction that these conversations should not be confined to specialists.

**Community.** We are building an ambassador network across London's universities and research institutions. It brings together early-career researchers, students, and professionals across technical, policy, and humanities disciplines who share the conviction that the questions AI raises belong to everyone, not just the people building the systems. Our ambassadors organise local discussions, connect their communities to our work, and help us reach the people who should be part of these conversations but aren't yet.

## Why London, why now

London is home to DeepMind, the Alan Turing Institute, UCL, Imperial, and one of the densest concentrations of AI research talent in the world. It is also the seat of one of the most active governments on AI regulation globally, and home to world-class faculties in law, philosophy, psychology, and the social sciences. The technical community, the policy community, and the public are all here, but they are not yet in the same room often enough, or with the right framing. That is what we provide.

And the timing is not incidental. The systems being built today, from large language models to autonomous agents to multimodal architectures, are developing faster than the frameworks meant to govern them. The window for shaping how these technologies are understood, regulated, and directed is open now. It will not stay open indefinitely.

## Who this is for

If you are a researcher working on large language models, reinforcement learning, or AI systems and you think the safety and governance questions deserve more than a paragraph in your paper's conclusion, this is for you.

If you are an engineering lead or founder building AI products and you want a serious space to think about what your systems will mean in five or ten years, this is for you.

If you are a student in computer science, law, psychology, philosophy, policy, or any field and you want to engage with these questions before your career choices are already made, this is for you.

If you are a policymaker trying to regulate technology that moves faster than legislation and you need better technical interlocutors, this is for you.

And if you are simply someone who recognises that AI will reshape the conditions of your life and you want to understand what is happening and have a voice in how it unfolds, this is for you too.

## Our commitment

We begin small, with intellectual seriousness as our founding principle. We do not pretend to have answers to questions that remain genuinely open. But we believe that the people building, governing, studying, and living with AI all have a stake in its direction, and that understanding must precede action.

The future is not a forecast. It is a design problem. And design problems require more than designers. They require everyone who will live with the result.
